# OVERLORD REIGN Cross-Civilization, Magic, Death, and Ghost Decisions

Status: PROJECT DECISION MIRROR

Date: 2026-09-11

Authority: mirrors explicit OVERLORD REIGN decisions. Numbered project canon files remain authoritative.

## Inter-civilization relations

Status: CANON DESIGN / PLANNED IMPLEMENTATION

Vanilla and source-mod inter-civilization relationships remain intact where they already exist.

Overlord Quests must not maintain a continuously evaluated diplomacy matrix across all civilizations.

Additional inter-civilization relationships become mechanically relevant only through authored quests that explicitly involve those civilizations.

Disposition with one civilization does not automatically propagate to a third civilization unless a specific quest authors that consequence.

Villager and Illager hostility remains an explicit standing exception because it already exists as a world relationship independently of the quest system.

## Conflict manipulation

Status: CANON DESIGN

The Overlord may broker, exploit, manipulate, prolong, redirect, or profit from conflicts between civilizations without becoming a conventional permanent ally of either side.

Authored multi-faction quests may support outcomes such as:

- proxy conflict;
- selective aid;
- forced truce;
- tribute or extortion from both parties;
- betrayal;
- one-sided subjugation;
- mutual subjugation where technically meaningful;
- selective destruction;
- engineered mutual destruction.

The practical ceiling is determined by what the quest/runtime systems can represent cleanly. No autonomous geopolitical simulation is required.

## Good Mana and Evil Mana terminology

Status: CANON PRESENTATION RULE

Good Mana and Evil Mana are real metaphysical polarities.

The formal terminology is used primarily by competent magic users, scholars, Gnarl, magical institutions, and other people with enough knowledge to describe the distinction precisely.

Ordinary people may instead use cultural language such as dark magic, holy magic, corruption, blessings, curses, or equivalent local terms.

## Social prevalence of magic

Status: CANON WORLD RULE

Minor supernatural knowledge is widespread enough to be part of ordinary life in a heavily magical world.

Genuinely competent magical practitioners remain notable rather than universally mundane.

Their prevalence is civilization-specific. A culture such as the Umvuthana may treat magic as an ordinary part of society, while another civilization may have only rare specialists.

The modpack's broad player access to magic does not imply that every ordinary NPC is a trained mage.

## Great Cataclysm memory

Status: CANON WORLD RULE

The Great Cataclysm is remembered more broadly than the exact details of individual ancient Overlords because it physically transformed the world and ended an age.

Its precise details have degraded over centuries.

Florian Greenheart's responsibility is specialist or recovered historical knowledge rather than universal common knowledge.

Different civilizations may preserve different interpretations, legends, warnings, or political uses of the Cataclysm.

## Magical taboo

Status: CANON DESIGN RULE

There is no universal morality chart assigning one fixed social judgment to every school or discipline of magic.

Taboo depends on civilization, religion, circumstance, and the specific act performed.

Necromancy, Blood magic, Biomancy, Theurgy, and other disciplines may be treated very differently by different populations.

Biomancy in particular should not be declared universally forbidden merely because Gnarl's use of it is morally questionable.

Universal prohibitions should be established only when source lore or a later explicit decision justifies them.

## Resurrection and permanent death

Status: CANON

True resurrection of ordinary sapient beings is exceptional and difficult.

It is not a routine service and must not be assumed available merely because the modpack contains resurrection-like mechanics.

Minion resurrection remains its own established special system.

Quest writing should treat the death of an ordinary named NPC as permanent unless that specific story explicitly establishes a valid resurrection mechanism.

Death therefore retains narrative weight.

## Ghosts and authored post-death persistence

Status: CANON DESIGN / PLANNED IMPLEMENTATION

Some dead NPCs may persist as ghosts, but ghost persistence is not automatic for every death.

Authored NPC deaths and quests may deliberately create a ghost path.

The current installed instance contains:

```text
ghosts-forge-1.20.1-1.3.3.jar
```

The installed Ghosts mod provides ghost entities, but its documented native behavior does not itself establish automatic conversion of dead NPCs into their personal ghosts.

Therefore Overlord Quests or a narrow compatibility layer must own the authored death-to-ghost bridge.

For an authored ghost:

- the ghost should carry the narrative identity of the dead NPC;
- its dialogue and quest behavior should derive from that NPC's established personality, history, relationships, and death circumstances;
- it may open an alternate post-death quest path instead of restoring the dead NPC to life;
- it must be truly unkillable, including by the Overlord;
- ordinary ambient ghosts from the Ghosts mod do not automatically receive this quest-critical immortality;
- authored quest ghosts should not be generated for every random NPC death, avoiding uncontrolled permanent entity accumulation.

Quest ghosts can deliberately echo the irritating, persistent ghost characters of the Overlord series when personality supports that tone.

Death remains death. A ghost is the continued presence of the dead person's spirit, not a resurrected living NPC.

## Soul, Lifeforce, identity, and resurrection

Status: CANON

Possessing or manipulating a soul does not automatically provide a perfect resurrectable copy of the dead person.

Soul, Lifeforce, personal identity, ghost persistence, undeath, and resurrection are related but non-equivalent concepts.

Raw Lifeforce may preserve usable vital essence without preserving the complete personal identity necessary for true resurrection.

Detailed soul-manipulation limits remain UNKNOWN until a specific Eidolon, necromancy, or soul-focused quest requires them.

This minimum rule prevents routine soul mechanics from trivializing permanent death.

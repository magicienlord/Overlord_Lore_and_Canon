# OVERLORD Franchise Peoples, Factions, and Cultures

Status: PRIMARY-SOURCE SYNTHESIS

Date: 2026-09-11

Authority: franchise reference only. This file records source-supported social and political traits. It deliberately separates a people's demonstrated behavior from insults, propaganda, or stereotypes voiced by Gnarl, enemies, or ambient NPCs.

## Source-discipline rule

A line such as Gnarl calling Gnomes pests or Dwarves drunkards is characterization of Gnarl unless independent evidence supports the underlying claim.

By contrast, a map objective, neutral system string, multiple independent speakers, or direct cultural behavior can establish a broader fact.

## Humans / peasants of the old lands

Evidence: `PRIMARY-EXPLICIT`, culturally heterogeneous.

Overlord I contains ordinary human farming communities, village guards, refugees, enslaved workers, city populations, soldiers, clergy, and nobles. They are not presented as one unified political civilization.

### Spree and the Mellow Hills

The Spree population is agricultural and village-based. Halfling raids displace farmers, seize farms and food, create work camps, and drive refugees into Spree.

The village can react materially to the Overlord's actions, including rescue, food recovery, fear, hostility, and changing judgments of his rule.

This is useful quest precedent for settlement state emerging from concrete events rather than an abstract reputation number.

### Heaven's Peak

Heaven's Peak is a more urban and militarized human center affected by plague, religious authority, soldiers, refugees, and corruption around Sir William.

The source therefore does not support treating all human settlements as culturally interchangeable.

## Halflings

Evidence: `PRIMARY-EXPLICIT` core facts; stereotypes treated cautiously.

Overlord I establishes a Halfling society centered in the Mellow Hills region under Melvin Underbelly.

Direct source facts include:

- Halfling forces seize human farms and food;
- they operate work camps holding human captives;
- they occupy a distinct village/domain;
- their forces can fight effectively despite their small stature;
- Melvin returned from earlier adventures as a celebrated hero before his appetite and rule became destructive;
- the Tower Heart was being misused in their territory to grow oversized pumpkins.

Food, appetite, feasting, kitchens, and gluttony are persistent thematic elements of the Halfling material. Some extreme statements about what Halflings will eat are hostile human jokes rather than neutral ethnography.

The source supports Halflings as a distinct people, not merely short human villagers.

## Elves

Evidence: `PRIMARY-EXPLICIT`.

Elves persist across both games and are strongly associated with magic, the Mother Goddess, nature, sacred places, and Sanctuaries.

### Overlord I

Evernight Forest is explicitly described as once filled with Elven magic.

Elven communities and spirits invoke the Mother Goddess. The corrupted state of Evernight is connected to Oberon's failure and magical/natural catastrophe.

Dwarven conquest later includes captured Elves, forced labor, theft of a Mother Goddess statue, and coerced construction of a temple around that stolen sacred object.

### Overlord II

The source treats magical aptitude as expected among Elves. Florian's lack of magic is presented as an abnormality for which other Elves mocked him.

Everlight is described as an ancient Elven retreat and retains strong Elven cultural presence.

Elven communities invoke the Mother Goddess, maintain Sanctuaries, and organize protection for magical and natural creatures.

An Elf commune also exists near Nordberg, showing that Elven social organization is not limited to one kingdom or city.

### Cultural caution

The source strongly associates Elves with Light magic and nature, but it does not establish that every Elf is morally good, politically unified, or a formal priest of the Mother Goddess.

## Dwarves

Evidence: `PRIMARY-EXPLICIT`.

The Dwarves inhabit the Golden Hills and possess a recognizable state/domain under King Goldo during Overlord I.

Direct source facts include:

- extensive gold mining;
- underground construction and fortified halls;
- a major brewing industry;
- guarded brewing rituals;
- a large Brewery running through the mountain;
- a Royal Halls complex;
- an Arcanium mine;
- territorial military power;
- enslavement of Elves;
- theft/appropriation of the Mother Goddess statue and forced construction around it.

Gnarl states that the center of the Dwarven brewing operation is so important that their empire is built upon the beverage. Even allowing for Gnarl's comic phrasing, the Brewery is objectively a major strategic installation and its destruction is treated as a decisive blow.

Gold is also structurally central. Goldo is explicitly obsessed with accumulating it, and Raising Hell continues the Dwarven/Goldo association with gold into the Golden Abyss.

### Cultural caution

Do not flatten all Dwarves into Goldo's personal greed. The source establishes a gold-rich, brewing-heavy, militarized Dwarven state and a greedy king. It does not prove every individual Dwarf shares Goldo's psychology.

## Gnomes

Evidence: `PRIMARY-EXPLICIT` limited facts; much surrounding dialogue is prejudicial.

Overlord II establishes Gnomes as a distinct small people with villages and holes/burrows.

Source-supported facts include:

- a Gnome village exists in the Wasteland/Sanctuary material;
- Gnomes use or possess underground holes that can be closed by sending Salamanders into them;
- a Spell Stone is said to belong to the Gnomes;
- Empire processing dialogue classifies Gnomes as having very little magic and as being unsuitable for the Arena.

Gnarl and Empire characters routinely express contempt for Gnomes. Those insults demonstrate social prejudice and comic tone, not objective proof that Gnomes are inherently vermin.

The source is presently too thin to reconstruct a detailed Gnome political culture.

## The Glorious Empire

Evidence: `PRIMARY-EXPLICIT`.

The Glorious Empire is the dominant human imperial power in Overlord II under Emperor Solarius.

### Political and military characteristics

The Empire has:

- Empire City as its center;
- governors and officials in subject/conquered settlements;
- organized soldiers and elite Imperial Guards;
- Sentinels used to detect magical beings;
- anti-magic infrastructure;
- Eradicators and facilities involved in processing magical creatures;
- an Arena used for spectacle, punishment, and combat;
- propaganda and public loyalty centered on Solarius;
- tourism, commerce, and social attraction sufficient that some provincial NPCs admire or aspire to visit Empire City.

### Anti-magic policy

The Empire systematically persecutes, captures, cleanses, or kills magical beings and magic users.

This policy is not merely military expediency. It forms part of the regime's public identity in a world traumatized by the Great Cataclysm and magical plague.

### Social complexity

Ambient dialogue shows that ordinary people can simultaneously fear, resent, admire, depend on, or aspire toward the Empire.

The Empire should therefore not be written as a population in which every civilian holds one identical ideology. The regime is oppressive, but its society includes supporters, opportunists, critics, tourists, workers, soldiers, slum residents, and provincial subjects.

## Sanctuaries and the Soldiers of the Sanctuary

Evidence: `PRIMARY-EXPLICIT`.

Sanctuaries are protected places associated with magical and natural beings.

Florian Greenheart introduces the Soldiers of the Sanctuary as protectors of the Sanctuaries and of creatures of magic and nature.

The Last Sanctuary is explicitly described as a place where creatures of Light magic can exist safely.

Queen Fay is closely tied to the Last Sanctuary, and source dialogue treats her life/power as materially connected to it.

The Sanctuary network is therefore both a refuge system and a political/military resistance structure against the Empire's anti-magic campaign.

It should not automatically be treated as synonymous with all Elven society, because the source describes its mission more broadly around magical and natural creatures.

## Minions

Evidence: `PRIMARY-EXPLICIT`.

Minions are a distinct people/species organized around service to an Overlord, color-based capabilities, Hives, Lifeforce, Gates, and the institutional memory represented by Gnarl.

They are not source-equivalent to goblins.

Their detailed biology, souls, Hives, and Netherworld infrastructure are maintained in `03_MINIONS_AND_NETHERWORLD.md`.

## Wraiths and the Evil dead

Evidence: `PRIMARY-EXPLICIT`, but not established as a normal civilization.

Raising Hell identifies Wraiths as spirits of the Evil dead inhabiting the Abyss material.

The current source does not support treating Wraiths as an ordinary surface people with settlements, economy, and diplomacy comparable to Dwarves or Elves.

## Political relationships directly established

The source supports several concrete historical conflicts:

- Halfling forces versus Spree/Mellow Hills humans;
- Dwarven state versus Elves, including enslavement and religious theft;
- Glorious Empire versus magical beings and Sanctuary forces;
- Overlord/Minions versus each of these groups at different points depending on campaign goals;
- internal player-choice outcomes can move settlements between destruction, domination, enslavement, or other authored conditions.

These historical relationships should not automatically be copied into OVERLORD REIGN's current-day diplomacy after several hundred years.

## Relevance to OVERLORD REIGN civilizations

Status: SOURCE CONSTRAINT / DESIGN INPUT, not automatic adoption.

REIGN already intends a broader eleven-civilization disposition layer. Only some of those peoples have direct equivalents in this franchise corpus.

Strong franchise precedents exist for:

- human settlements;
- Elves;
- Dwarves;
- Gnomes;
- Minions as the Overlord's own people;
- magical refuge/resistance organizations;
- imperial human states.

Minecraft/modded peoples such as Gnumus, Kobolds, Ribbits, Sea Dwellers, Piglins, Umvuthana, and others require REIGN-specific cultural integration rather than fabricated claims that they existed in the original games.
